## Cover

**Kicker:** Talanta, 74-patient pilot
**Headline:** A breath test caught early-stage lung cancer with **93% accuracy**, using only two chemical markers.

## Slide 1 - What it means for you

**Title:** If these two markers hold up in bigger trials, screening could shift from imaging to a simple breath test.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

The authors suggest the two-marker panel could support low-cost, point-of-care screening. No scan, no blood draw, just exhaled air.

The model detected stage I disease in this cohort. External testing in larger groups is needed before clinical use.

## Slide 2 - The result

**Title:** A machine learning model reading breath chemicals distinguished 74 early-stage lung cancer patients from 75 healthy controls.

The model hit an area under the curve of 0.991, with 93.3% correct on both counts. A simpler model using two chemicals reached an AUC of 0.708.

The team analyzed 562 volatile organic compounds in breath samples.

## Slide 3 - How it works

**Title:** Nineteen breath chemicals changed in a clear trend as disease progressed from no cancer to stage IA to IB.

Two compounds emerged as network-central markers. The team used nested cross-validation and a separate test set to avoid overfitting.

The authors note that early metabolic shifts appear in breath before symptoms. The study was one center, so results need confirmation.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link between these breath chemicals and cancer.
- Sample size was 74 patients and 75 controls. Worth replicating in trials at multiple centers before anyone changes screening practice.
