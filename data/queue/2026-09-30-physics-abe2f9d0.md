## Slide 1 - What it means for you

**Title:** The team uses the LUX-ZEPLIN detector's unusual signal to explore two different particle models of dark matter.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

One candidate event doesn't confirm anything. But it does let physicists test whether their theories can explain what the detector saw.

This early paper reports two supersymmetric scenarios that could match the signal, each predicting different patterns in future data.

## Slide 2 - The result

**Title:** The LZ experiment recently recorded a nuclear-recoil candidate at an energy of about 248 kiloelectronvolts.

The team explored two interpretations. One involves a neutralino particle around 500 GeV that scatters elastically, with specific coupling strengths below current limits.

The other involves a pseudo-Dirac Higgsino with a mass splitting of a few hundred keV.

## Slide 3 - How it works

**Title:** The two mechanisms predict distinct recoil spectra, meaning future detections could distinguish between them.

In the elastic case, scattering occurs through a diagonal axial Z-boson coupling. In the inelastic case, a pseudo-Dirac Higgsino scatters endothermically with a specific mass splitting.

Both scenarios can avoid current experimental constraints while remaining consistent with observed dark-matter abundance.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint. It has not been peer reviewed, so no independent expert has checked the work yet.
- A single candidate event is not a confirmed detection. It could be background noise or a statistical fluctuation.
- The models are theoretical interpretations. Whether either one actually describes dark matter requires more data from this and other experiments.
