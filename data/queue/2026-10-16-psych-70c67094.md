## Cover

**Kicker:** Lancet Regional Health Americas
**Headline:** Locking up a parent may come with **health costs for children** that reverse when the parent gets out.

## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** Immigration enforcement policies affect more than the detained parent. The children at home may bear health costs too.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

If parental detention is linked to measurable declines in child health, policymakers need to weigh those consequences when setting enforcement priorities.

This study suggests those health effects may reverse once the parent is released.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Children's reported health scores dropped by 17 points during parental detention, then bounced back after release.

Families of eight detained immigrants reported on child health using a standardized scale. Before detention, scores sat at the U.S. average. During detention, they fell two standard deviations below it.

After release, scores returned to baseline.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** Researchers surveyed families of people detained by ICE in 2020–21 and later released on bond.

Forty-nine families with school-aged children answered questions about health before, during, and after detention. About one in six children could not get mental health treatment during detention.

More than half of families reported food insecurity.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else about the detention period could explain the drop in health scores.
- Only eight families took part. That is small enough that a bigger study could find a very different result.
- This research was partly funded by industry, which is worth keeping in mind when you read the findings.
