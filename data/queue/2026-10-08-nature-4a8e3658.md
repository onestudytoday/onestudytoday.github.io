## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** Rising sea temperatures are pushing tropical bacteria into European coastlines with no coordinated tracking system.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Vibrio bacteria cause wound and foodborne infections. They used to stay in tropical waters. Warming seas are bringing them north.

Recent outbreaks in the Baltic and North Seas show Europe is unprepared. Surveillance is patchy and public warnings are limited.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Vibrio infections are increasing in frequency and moving to higher latitudes as extreme weather reshapes coastal waters.

Vibrio vulnificus and V. parahaemolyticus now appear in regions that were previously too cold. Climate change is expanding suitable habitat for these pathogens.

Europe needs integrated monitoring that combines environmental tracking, genomic surveillance, and public health communication.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** Vibrio species thrive in warm seawater and infect people through open wounds or contaminated shellfish.

The bacteria are recognized as a sentinel of climate-linked infectious disease. But no unified European framework exists to monitor outbreaks or warn the public.

Coordinated preparedness will protect health systems and coastal communities as waters continue warming.
