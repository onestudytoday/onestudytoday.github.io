## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** The fraction of galaxies emitting detectable light at specific wavelengths may tell us when and how reionization happened.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

After the Big Bang, neutral hydrogen filled the universe and blocked ultraviolet light. Early galaxies ionized it, creating clear bubbles that merged.

Tracking which galaxies show Lyman-alpha light lets researchers map that transition and estimate bubble sizes.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** The team reports the universe was 57 percent neutral at redshift 8 and 93 percent neutral at redshift 11.

They compared JWST Lyman-alpha fractions with simulation predictions. The match was good, with reduced chi-squared between 0.4 and 1.2.

At redshift 7, the data suggest roughly 38 percent neutral hydrogen by mass, rising steeply at earlier times.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** Lyman-alpha photons scatter off neutral hydrogen, so fewer galaxies show the line when more of the medium is neutral.

The researchers used the THESAN simulation and a semi-analytic model. They calibrated intrinsic emission using data from redshift 6.

The fraction of galaxies with detectable Lyman-alpha drops sharply at higher redshifts, tracking the rising neutral fraction.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint and has not been peer reviewed yet, so no independent expert has checked the work.
- The bounds are wide and rely on models of both galaxy emission and reionization that carry their own assumptions.
