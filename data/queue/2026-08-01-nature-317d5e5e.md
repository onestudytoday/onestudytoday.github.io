## Cover

**Kicker:** Bioelectrochemistry • field-tested
**Headline:** A new sensor can detect **starfish DNA in seawater at femtogram levels**, low enough to catch outbreaks early.

## Slide 1 - Why you should care

**Title:** If coral reefs could be monitored this sensitively, outbreaks might be caught before they spread.

**Basis:** inferred    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Crown-of-thorns starfish destroy coral reefs when populations explode. Current monitoring is too slow and too coarse to catch outbreaks early.

This sensor could detect trace DNA in seawater before the starfish become visible, giving managers time to act.

## Slide 2 - What the data says

**Title:** The sensor detected starfish DNA down to 2.02 femtograms per microlitre and matched reference results in field samples.

The team used magnetic beads and DNA amplification to concentrate target sequences before electrochemical readout. Detection was linear across six orders of magnitude.

Field samples from the Xisha Islands matched droplet digital PCR, the gold standard, with no statistical difference.

## Slide 3 - The assumption

**Title:** You would assume electrochemical sensors need complex surface chemistry that makes them fragile and inconsistent.

Most DNA sensors immobilize probes directly on electrodes. That creates uneven coverage and poor stability.

This design does the chemistry in solution, then pulls the complexes onto the electrode with magnets. Simpler, and more consistent.

## Caveats

- This was a proof-of-concept study with lab-spiked samples and a limited number of field samples from one location.
- The abstract does not report how the sensor performs with DNA from closely related starfish species or other marine organisms.
