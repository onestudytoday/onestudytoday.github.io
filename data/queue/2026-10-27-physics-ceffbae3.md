## Slide 1 - Why you should care

**Title:** The team reports the first clear signs of how electrons lose their quantum nature when passing through metal.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

This settles a two-decade search for a specific quantum test. It also shows that quantum Brownian motion drives how electrons lose their quantum nature.

That matters for tiny quantum devices and for the sharpness limits of electron microscopes.

## Slide 2 - What the data says

**Title:** The test measured how electrons moving through metal channels lose quantum behavior through contact with the metal.

The results matched predictions from quantum loss theory. The researchers could tune the loss smoothly, showing quantum Brownian motion is the mechanism.

This is the first real-world version of a proposal made twenty years ago.

## Slide 3 - The assumption

**Title:** Motion-based quantum loss is central to the quantum-to-classical shift, but the mechanism was not confirmed in free electrons.

Quantum objects can exist in multiple states at once. Larger objects do not. The shift happens when a quantum system touches its surroundings.

The double-slit test shows this with electrons, but proving what drives it needed controlled systems that only now exist.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint. It has not been peer reviewed yet, so no independent expert has checked the work.
- The study tests a specific quantum mechanism in a lab system. Real-world uses for devices and microscopes remain to be shown.
