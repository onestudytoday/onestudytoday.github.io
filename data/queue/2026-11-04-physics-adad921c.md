## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Basis:** inferred    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Quantum computers promise to search certain structures faster than ordinary machines. But this early paper suggests a floor.

That could mean some pattern-detection tasks will always be expensive, even in a quantum future.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** The team reports that finding triangles in a network requires at least N to the power 13/12 queries.

For graphs with N vertices, the best quantum algorithms use about N to the power 5/4 queries. The new lower bound is N to the power 13/12.

That narrows the gap between possible and necessary.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** Triangle finding means detecting when three nodes in a graph all connect to each other.

It is a benchmark problem in quantum algorithm design. For years, the lower bound was just N queries, the cost of unstructured search.

The work compares graphs with a planted triangle to random triangle-free graphs.

## Caveats
