## Cover

**Kicker:** Journal of Affective Disorders
**Headline:** **Regular cannabis use is linked to more than double the risk** of later developing bipolar disorder.

## Slide 1 - Why you should care

**Title:** Cannabis is often seen as low-risk, but this meta-analysis suggests it may be tied to serious mood instability.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

The authors say routine screening for cannabis use and prevention strategies to delay or reduce use may be warranted.

Bipolar disorder affects roughly two percent of the population, and spotting modifiable risk factors before onset is a priority.

## Slide 2 - What the data says

**Title:** Researchers pooled nineteen studies tracking nearly seven million people without prior bipolar disorder.

People who used cannabis had 2.46 times the risk of developing bipolar disorder compared to non-users. They also had 2.11 times the risk of manic or hypomanic symptoms.

The studies were observational, following participants over time.

## Slide 3 - The assumption

**Title:** Cannabis is widely used and often framed as relatively benign, especially compared to alcohol or other substances.

Earlier reviews included few studies or lumped different bipolar outcomes together, making it hard to draw specific conclusions.

This review separated incident bipolar disorder from manic symptoms, giving a clearer picture for each outcome.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link.
- The review could not control for all confounding factors across the nineteen studies, like family history or other substance use.
- Most participants were in their twenties, so the findings may not apply equally to older or younger groups.
