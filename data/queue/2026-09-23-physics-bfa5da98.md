## Cover

**Kicker:** arXiv preprint, not reviewed
**Headline:** **Early JWST data may suggest galaxies ignited suddenly** at a specific moment, not gradually as expected

## Slide 1 - What they found

**Title:** The team reports that stellar ages in high-redshift galaxies converge to zero by z=15, with no earlier star formation.

JWST has found bright young galaxies out to z=14.5. But deeper searches for fainter, earlier ones now turn up only misidentified objects.

The stars in these distant galaxies all appear to have formed recently, with no older populations.

## Slide 2 - Why this matters

**Title:** The paper argues this sharp cutoff confirms a prediction of Wave Dark Matter models.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Standard dark matter predicts a gradual Cosmic Dawn. Galaxies should appear progressively fainter as you look back.

Instead, this early paper suggests a late, abrupt transition. That matches ultra-light boson dark matter, not heavy particles.

## Slide 3 - The mechanism

**Title:** In Wave Dark Matter, quantum pressure resists collapse until gas clouds reach 4 billion solar masses at z=15.

The team calculates a boson mass of 2.2 × 10⁻²² eV. That figure is similar to independent estimates from lensing and dwarf galaxies.

The highest-redshift galaxy luminosities converge to the predicted scale, not the lower values standard models expect.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint. It has not been peer reviewed, so no independent expert has checked the work yet.
- The interpretation depends on stellar age estimates, which carry large uncertainties at these extreme distances.
- Wave Dark Matter remains a minority hypothesis. Most cosmologists work with cold dark matter made of heavy particles.
