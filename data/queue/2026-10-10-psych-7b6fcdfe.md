## Cover

**Kicker:** Journal of Experimental Child Psychology
**Headline:** Even preschoolers can invent a memory trick on the fly, **but they may not know why it works**

## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** Young children can create strategies to solve impossible memory problems, but understanding the strategy itself comes later.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Kids as young as three figured out how to use environmental cues to remember where a toy was hidden when the task was designed to be unsolvable without a trick.

When researchers changed the setup slightly, preschoolers struggled to adapt. They could execute the strategy but seemed unaware of why it worked.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Children aged three to nine successfully used a uniquely colored cup to mark their preferred toy's location during mixing.

Kids assigned toys to four opaque cups, one uniquely colored, before the cups were shuffled. High success rates across ages meant children placed the preferred toy in the marked cup.

Older children reached proficiency faster. All children made fewer errors with practice.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** Researchers gave no instructions, forcing children to invent their own approach to guarantee they could find their favorite toy.

Four opaque cups were presented, one a different color. Children distributed toys among them, then cups were mixed and re-presented.

Without using the colored cup as a marker, children had to guess among three identical cups.

## Caveats

- The study did not report sample size or detailed demographic information, limiting assessment of how representative the findings are.
- Preschoolers failed follow-up variations that required setting up the task differently, suggesting the original success may have been partly accidental.
