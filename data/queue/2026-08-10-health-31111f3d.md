## Cover

**Kicker:** Biomaterials · mice + cells
**Headline:** A targeted nanogel protected mouse hearts from **radiation damage during cancer treatment** by restoring RNA.

## Slide 1 - What it could mean

**Title:** If this approach translates, it could prevent heart damage in patients receiving radiotherapy for chest cancers.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Radiation to the chest can injure the heart, limiting how much therapy doctors can safely give. The study shows a nanogel carrying circNCX1 RNA preserved heart function in mice.

The gel was given before radiation and targeted heart tissue, leaving other organs unaffected.

## Slide 2 - Results

**Title:** Mice given the circNCX1 nanogel before a 20 Gy heart-focused radiation dose maintained cardiac function for 12 weeks.

Treated mice showed preserved heart function, less muscle scarring, and fewer dying heart cells compared to controls. Blood tests and tissue analysis found no damage to other major organs.

The nanogel accumulated rapidly in the heart after IV injection.

## Slide 3 - What they did

**Title:** Researchers built a nanogel carrying a circular RNA and tagged it with peptides to guide it to heart tissue.

The team first found that radiation suppressed circNCX1, a circular RNA that normally blocks cell death signals. They packaged circNCX1 into a polymer gel coated with targeting peptides.

Male C57BL/6J mice received the gel by vein before chest radiation. Heart function was tracked for three months.

## Caveats

- This was done in cells in a dish and mice, not humans. Most findings in cells and mice do not carry over to people.
- The nanogel was given before radiation, not after damage had already occurred. Timing may matter clinically.
- The study tracked outcomes for 12 weeks. Long-term effects and whether protection lasts beyond that window are unknown.
