**Headline:** Gas clouds near black holes **echo nuclear flares a decade later**, the team reports from galactic cores.

## Slide 1 - Why you should care

**Title:** Compact gas near black holes may form an intermediate response layer on decade timescales.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

The team reports that ionized gas a few light-years from a black hole responds to nuclear outbursts years after the event.

That would place this gas between fast-responding dust and the much larger emission regions, a middle layer not documented before.

## Slide 2 - What the data says

**Title:** Nineteen galactic nuclei showed narrow emission-line flux more than doubling over twelve to twenty years.

The team analyzed 473 galactic nuclei with signs of recent brightening. In stacked spectra, one oxygen line increased by 229% while lower-energy lines stayed flat or fell.

For thirteen sources with infrared outbursts, the infrared energy correlated strongly with the line change.

## Slide 3 - Why the difference

**Title:** The selective increase in high-ionization lines points to a harder spectrum and higher ionization, not simple rescaling.

Lower-ionization lines did not follow the same pattern, ruling out a uniform brightness increase. Models fit the data when the ionizing spectrum becomes harder.

The timescale and mid-infrared data constrain the gas to one and a half to five light-years from the nucleus.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint. No independent expert has checked the methods or conclusions yet.
- The study found a pattern linking infrared outbursts and emission-line changes, not a cause. Something else could explain the link.
