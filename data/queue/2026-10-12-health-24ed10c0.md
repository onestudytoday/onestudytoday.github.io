## Cover

**Kicker:** Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience
**Headline:** **Brain scans before treatment** may predict who recovers from internet gaming disorder.

## Slide 1 - What it could mean

**Title:** A brain pattern predicted symptom change before treatment started, no matter which therapy people received.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

If brain scans can show who will respond to treatment, doctors could tailor care earlier. The pattern worked across three different therapies.

This moves closer to custom treatment plans based on brain markers, not trial and error.

## Slide 2 - Results

**Title:** One brain network predicted 40% of symptom change; another predicted 48%.

Researchers looked at resting-state brain scans from 90 people with internet gaming disorder. Whole-brain patterns predicted individual symptom change across all three therapies.

The positive network showed stronger prediction. Both models were tested on an external dataset.

## Slide 3 - What they did

**Title:** Ninety people with internet gaming disorder received one of three therapies.

The therapies were mindfulness meditation, approach bias training, or retrieval-aversive training. Resting-state fMRI scans were taken before treatment began.

The model was tested on how well it predicted individual symptom change across the three different therapy types.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link between brain patterns and treatment response.
- All participants had internet gaming disorder. Whether these patterns predict outcomes in other conditions is unknown.
