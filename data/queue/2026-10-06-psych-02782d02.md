## Cover

**Kicker:** Cognition · Comparative data
**Headline:** **Nested thinking isn't only human** - it shows up in crows and monkeys, just less reliably.

## Slide 1 - What it means for you

**Title:** The mental skill behind language and tool use appears across species, not just in us.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Nested reasoning - sorting information in layers - may emerge gradually across development and evolution rather than appearing suddenly in humans.

If this holds, the line between human and animal cognition is less sharp than we thought.

## Slide 2 - The result

**Title:** Adults showed reliable nested reasoning, while children, crows, and monkeys varied by task.

Researchers tested US and Bolivian adults, 3-to-6-year-old children, rhesus monkeys, and carrion crows on nested reasoning tasks with different levels of difficulty.

All groups showed evidence of nested reasoning, but non-human animals and children performed less reliably depending on context.

## Slide 3 - How it works

**Title:** The team used Bayesian modeling to compare performance across visual and conceptual reasoning tasks.

They analyzed both new data and previously published results. Task difficulty and whether stimuli were visual or semantic both influenced how well each group used nested thinking.

The findings suggest nested reasoning is flexible and context-dependent, not all-or-none.

## Caveats

- The study combined new data with previously published results, so methods varied across the groups tested.
- Individual variation was high within each population, meaning group-level patterns don't predict any single crow or child.
- The tasks were lab-based, so we don't know how nested reasoning plays out in natural behavior.
