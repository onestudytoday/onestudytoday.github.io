## Cover

**Kicker:** arXiv preprint, early paper
**Headline:** **A rare planetary system kept six worlds in sync** even with a giant trying to pull them apart.

## Slide 1 - What it means for you

**Title:** Planet formation can produce stable setups we thought impossible to keep long-term.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

The team reports that TOI-178 is the only known system where many planets in sync survived with a massive outer companion nearby.

The inner six planets have orbited in lockstep for billions of years, despite the giant twenty-one times Jupiter's mass.

## Slide 2 - The result

**Title:** The team found a massive brown dwarf orbiting at 6.9 AU, with 21 Jupiter masses.

Five of the six inner planets sit in a 2:4:6:9:12 resonant chain within 0.15 AU of the star. The newly found companion lies several AU out.

Simulations show the inner chain stays stable despite the outer companion's pull, consistent with the system being several billion years old.

## Slide 3 - How it works

**Title:** The discovery came from data showing the star's wobble, plus imaging and velocity measures.

The researchers analyzed oddities in the Gaia and Hipparcos catalogs showing the star's position shifting over time. Combined with velocity data, this revealed a massive companion.

The inner chain's survival suggests it formed early and locked in before the outer orbit could break it.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint. It has not been peer reviewed, so no independent expert has checked the work yet.
- The mass and orbit parameters have wide error ranges, especially for the distance and orbit shape.
- The stability simulations are models. They show the system could survive, not that this is definitely how it formed.
