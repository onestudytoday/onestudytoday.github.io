# 2026-09-01-nature-94100347

## Cover

**Kicker:** Bioactive Materials · mice + pigs
**Headline:** A new hydrogel **sticks to the colon for three days**, keeping transplanted gut bacteria alive.

## Slide 1 - What it means for you

**Title:** If this works in people, fecal transplants could become far more effective at fixing gut bacteria problems.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Right now, transplanted bacteria get flushed out before they can colonize. Most don't survive the stomach or bowel movement.

This gel uses colon water to harden in place. It holds bacteria against the gut wall for over 72 hours.

## Slide 2 - The result

**Title:** The injectable gel stayed attached to mouse and pig colons for more than 72 hours while keeping microbiota alive.

Researchers injected a low-viscosity solution through the rectum. Contact with the wet colon triggered rapid gelation.

The gel formed strong bonds with the mucosal surface. It significantly improved outcomes in a mouse colitis model.

## Slide 3 - How it works

**Title:** Water from the colon wall triggers a chemical reaction that turns the liquid into a solid mesh.

The formula mixes two compounds in a biocompatible solvent. When tissue water enters, it causes rapid polymerization.

Chemical groups then form hydrogen bonds and covalent links with mucus. The gel conforms to the surface as new mucus secretes.

## Caveats

- This was done in mice and pigs, not humans. Most findings in mice and pigs do not carry over to people.
- The study tested the gel's ability to stick and keep bacteria alive, but long-term safety and efficacy in humans remain unknown.
