## Cover

**Kicker:** Clinical and translational radiation oncology
**Headline:** Doctors systematically **miss symptoms** that matter most to patients with brain cancer

## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** If clinicians alone decide what counts as a side effect, patients suffer symptoms no one is tracking or treating.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

This reveals a structural perception gap in neuro-oncology care. The symptoms doctors miss most are psychological distress and physical complaints not obviously tied to radiation.

Routine patient self-reports could catch what clinical exams overlook and shift care toward what patients experience.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Across 84% of symptoms tracked, patients reported worse severity than their doctors recorded during brain radiation treatment.

Researchers compared clinician assessments against patient questionnaires at three points during radiotherapy for brain metastases. Agreement was fair for worry and depression but almost perfect for vomiting and seizures.

The biggest gaps appeared for shortness of breath, fatigue, and sleep trouble.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** Sixty-three patients receiving brain radiation completed questionnaires at baseline, end of treatment, and first follow-up.

Doctors scored side effects using standardized toxicity criteria. Patients rated the same symptoms using quality-of-life scales designed for cancer and brain tumor patients.

The study generated 4,448 matched comparisons and measured agreement for each symptom.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link between clinician assessments and patient reports.
- The sample was sixty-three patients at centers participating in the study, which may not reflect all radiation oncology practices or patient populations.
- Agreement varied widely by symptom type, so the overall pattern may obscure important differences in how specific side effects are perceived.
