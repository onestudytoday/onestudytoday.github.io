## Cover

**Kicker:** Int. J. Endocrin. Metab. • Review
**Headline:** AI-powered apps **may help kids with diabetes track glucose and adjust insulin** without constant parental watch.

## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** Managing diabetes in childhood means round-the-clock decisions about food, insulin, and blood sugar.

**Basis:** inferred    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

AI-driven tools could shift some of that cognitive load from parent and child to software that learns patterns and prompts action.

If these tools work reliably in long-term studies, they might make daily care less exhausting and more precise.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Nineteen studies on pediatric diabetes apps showed AI tools clustering into five areas of focus.

Eight focused on glucose prediction and pattern spotting. Four evaluated insulin dosing and clinical decision support. Six looked at tailored education and behavioral coaching.

Five examined remote monitoring and telemedicine. Five primarily addressed ethical challenges, transparency, and rollout barriers.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** Researchers searched six databases for studies published between January 2010 and March 2025.

They screened ninety-seven articles. Nineteen met inclusion criteria for AI-driven digital tools designed for children with diabetes.

Two independent reviewers extracted data and assessed quality using three established appraisal frameworks.

## Caveats

- This is a scoping review, not a trial. It maps what has been studied, not whether the tools work in the long term.
- The authors call for rigorous assessments of clinical outcomes, data privacy, and algorithm performance before widespread use.
