## Cover

**Kicker:** JSES reviews, reports, techniques · 21 studies
**Headline:** Shoulder replacements **can skip the metal stem** without losing function or raising revision risk.

## Slide 1 - Why you should care

**Title:** A simpler implant design could mean shorter surgery and less blood loss for patients needing shoulder replacements.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

The stemless version takes less time to put in and causes less bleeding during surgery. Everything else that matters comes out the same.

That matters most for younger patients with good bone quality who want to preserve their natural anatomy.

## Slide 2 - What the data says

**Title:** A review of 19 studies found no difference in function, range of motion, or revision rates.

Shoulder scores after surgery, forward flexion, abduction, external rotation, and center of rotation were equivalent. Revision risk showed no difference either.

Stemless implants did show shorter surgery time and reduced blood loss compared to traditional stemmed designs.

## Slide 3 - The assumption

**Title:** The traditional stemmed implant has been the standard for decades. Removing the stem sounds risky.

Surgeons have relied on the metal stem extending into the bone for fixation and alignment. It sounds like removing a safety feature.

But this review suggests the metaphyseal bone can hold the implant securely on its own.

## Caveats

- This review combined results from 19 studies with different patient groups, implant brands, and follow-up lengths, which can hide important differences.
- The paper reports relative effects without always giving baseline rates, so a percentage difference may represent a small absolute change in real terms.
