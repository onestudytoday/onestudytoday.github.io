## Cover

**Kicker:** Eco-Environment & Health journal
**Headline:** **Climate warming is linked to rising toxic air pollution** and bigger health gaps between rich and poor nations.

## Slide 1 - What it could mean

**Title:** Warming may widen global inequality by raising toxic air exposure in low-income regions.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

A toxic gas linked to cancer and lung disease goes up as the planet heats. If this holds, people in Africa and South America face the steepest rises.

People in low-income countries could see exposure triple by century's end, while wealthier regions see smaller increases.

## Slide 2 - Results

**Title:** The toxic gas goes up by about 2.3% for every degree of warming, driven by plants and wildfires.

Researchers used satellite data and deep learning to project future exposure. Under a mid-range climate scenario, global exposure to risky levels roughly doubles by 2100.

One-fifth of people worldwide already face harmful levels today.

## Slide 3 - What they did

**Title:** The team trained a deep learning model on more than a decade of daily satellite observations.

They extracted patterns showing how the gas responds to heat and drought. Then they projected exposure under different warming scenarios through the end of the century.

The model linked increases mainly to higher emissions from plants and wildfires.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link between warming and the toxic gas levels.
- The projections rely on satellite observations and climate models, which carry uncertainty about future emissions and land use.
