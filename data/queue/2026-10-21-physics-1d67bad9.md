## Cover

**Kicker:** arXiv (preprint - not reviewed)
**Headline:** **Titan's mountains may explain** why the Huygens probe measured winds blowing the wrong way when it landed

## Slide 1 - Why you should care

**Title:** Large-scale topography might create local wind patterns that contradict a moon's overall circulation.

**Basis:** inferred    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Climate models consistently predict westward winds near Titan's surface. But Huygens measured eastward winds instead.

This early paper reports that mountains could force stationary waves that flip wind direction in some spots.

## Slide 2 - What the data says

**Title:** The team paired two simulations of Titan's atmosphere: one with topography, one without.

Only the version with mountains reproduced the eastward flow Huygens saw in the lowest kilometer.

The effect comes from overlapping wave patterns locked to the terrain. A wave with one peak around the moon provides the eastward push.

## Slide 3 - The assumption

**Title:** Titan's equator superrotates: the atmosphere spins faster than the surface beneath it.

That creates a westward wind at the ground everywhere, according to models. Huygens landed in 2005 and measured the opposite.

The team now argues that what Huygens saw was a local exception, not a problem with the models.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint. It has not been peer reviewed, so no independent expert has checked the work yet.
- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link between terrain and wind direction.
- The conclusions about why the probe measured eastward winds are our own extrapolation. Read the full paper for what the authors actually claim.
