## Cover

**Kicker:** arXiv preprint, early data
**Headline:** **Early black holes were bigger** than current models predict, suggesting they grew faster or started heavier.

## Slide 1 - What it means for you

**Title:** The team reports that current theories cannot explain how black holes got this massive this early.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Their models require either much heavier starting seeds or faster-than-expected growth. Both options would require rethinking how the first black holes formed.

This matters because black holes shaped galaxy formation.

## Slide 2 - The result

**Title:** The team measured black hole masses between four million and two hundred million solar masses at redshift eight.

They found twenty active black holes with broad emission lines in early galaxies. The population density is about ten times higher than at redshift five.

That growth rate matches an accretion of about ten percent of the Eddington limit.

## Slide 3 - How it works

**Title:** They searched over twenty thousand spectra from JWST for broad hydrogen-beta emission in high-redshift galaxies.

Broad emission lines indicate gas moving fast around a central mass. The width gives you the black hole mass directly.

All twenty sources were little red dots, compact galaxies with dusty colors.

## Caveats

- This is a preprint. It has not been peer reviewed, so no independent expert has checked the work yet.
- The black hole masses assume the broad lines come from gravitationally bound gas. Other mechanisms could mimic the signal.
- The sample relies on spectroscopic completeness corrections that depend on model assumptions about how these objects are distributed.
