# 2026-10-27-nature-d42d9ae0

## Cover

**Kicker:** Lancet Regional Health · 20-year cohort
**Headline:** **Extreme heat hits migrants harder** - a gap climate policy may need to address in arrival cities.

## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** Climate plans that treat everyone the same may leave migrants behind, widening a heat death gap.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

If migrants are more at risk from heat, then policies that ignore that gap could fail the people who need help most.

The authors say their results highlight the need to consider migrants for targeted climate policy, with a focus on cutting gaps where migrants arrive.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Internal migrants in Brazil were more likely to die during extreme heat than non-migrants, mainly from breathing-related causes.

Researchers linked 1.2 million migrant deaths and 3.7 million non-migrant deaths to weather data over twenty years. During extreme heat, breathing-related death risk rose 32 percent among migrants but only 6 percent among non-migrants.

For migrants who could not read, heat-related breathing risk rose 84 percent.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** They used a case-crossover design comparing each person's weather exposure on the day they died to other days.

The 100 Million Brazilian Cohort was linked to death records from 2000 to 2020. Internal migrants were people whose state of birth differed from where they lived.

The team compared extreme cold and extreme heat to the level with the lowest death risk, looking at effects over twenty days.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else about moving or where migrants live could explain the link.
- The numbers are relative changes. Without knowing baseline rates, an 84 percent rise might still be a small real-world shift.
- Funded in part by the Wellcome Trust, worth knowing when you read the result.
