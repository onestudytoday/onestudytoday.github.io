**Headline:** **Different sleep stages handle different kinds of memory** - and targeting the right one could sharpen recall.

## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** Future sleep tools may target specific brain rhythms to strengthen thinking and memory.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

The review suggests that mapping brain waves during sleep could yield markers for checking and improving recall. Dense recording combined with models may guide clinical tools.

That matters because sleep is already prescribed, but knowing which rhythm does what could make the advice more precise.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Deep sleep transfers new memories to long-term storage; REM sleep refines emotion and skill-based ones.

During NREM sleep, slow waves coordinate with spindles and ripples to move fragile hippocampal memories into the cortex for permanent storage.

In REM sleep, theta waves sync limbic circuits. The chemical environment promotes spine reshaping and strips emotion from memory content.

## Slide 3 - The mechanism

**Title:** Each sleep stage uses different brain rhythms and chemical environments to reshape synapses.

NREM slow waves create UP and DOWN states that let different brain regions communicate. Thalamic spindles couple with hippocampal ripples to replay events and transfer them.

REM sleep depletes monoamines but floods the brain with acetylcholine. That sparse firing allows spines to reshape and feeling to detach from memories.

## Caveats

- This is a synthesis review, not a new experiment - it summarizes existing findings rather than reporting fresh data.
- How different brain waves interact across sleep stages remains unclear, as does the role of glial cells in memory consolidation.
