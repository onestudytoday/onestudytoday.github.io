## Cover

**Kicker:** DEN open case report
**Headline:** One type of acid blocker **may shrink polyps where another grew them**, even with higher gastrin levels.

## Slide 1 - What it means for you

**Title:** If you developed stomach polyps on a PPI, switching drug classes might change their course.

**Basis:** inferred    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

Fundic gland polyps often go together with long-term proton pump inhibitor use. High gastrin from acid suppression has been blamed.

A newer acid blocker raised gastrin even higher. The polyps still shrank, suggesting the link is more than gastrin alone.

## Slide 2 - The result

**Title:** One woman's fundic gland polyps regressed after switching from rabeprazole to a PCAB, with no re-growth.

The polyps grew over seven years on the PPI rabeprazole. Four months after switching to vonoprazan, they had regressed markedly.

Gastrin stayed elevated during PCAB therapy—491.9 and 753.7 pg/mL—yet the polyps did not return.

## Slide 3 - How it works

**Title:** The timing is unclear, so regression may have started when the PPI was stopped, not when the PCAB began.

The woman briefly used an H2-receptor antagonist between drugs. Regression was first documented four months into PCAB use.

Long-term PCAB therapy was not linked to polyp re-enlargement, despite potent acid suppression and elevated gastrin.

## Caveats

- This is a single case report in one woman, not a controlled study across many people.
- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link.
- The exact timing of polyp regression is unknown, so the PCAB may not be solely responsible.
