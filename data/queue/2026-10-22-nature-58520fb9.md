**Headline:** **Wildfire smoke waves change how air pollution links to heart hospitalizations**, and averages may miss it.

## Slide 1 - What it could mean

**Title:** Long-term average air pollution may underestimate heart disease burden in areas hit by wildfire smoke waves.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

The link between years of exposure to fine particles and heart hospitalizations was weaker in places that experienced wildfire smoke waves.

Risk assessments that rely only on long-term averages may miss the full cardiovascular toll in smoke-affected regions.

## Slide 2 - Results

**Title:** Moving from 5 to 9 micrograms per cubic meter raised heart hospitalization risk more in years without smoke waves.

In ZIP codes with no wildfire smoke waves, the jump in two-year average pollution was linked to 9 percent higher risk of heart hospitalizations.

In ZIP codes with smoke waves, the same increase was linked to only 4 percent higher risk.

## Slide 3 - What they did

**Title:** The team tracked 65.2 million Medicare beneficiaries aged 65 and older from 2017 to 2022 across the contiguous U.S.

Daily fine particle concentrations were estimated at 1 kilometer resolution and matched to each beneficiary's residential ZIP code.

Smoke waves were defined as two or more consecutive days with wildfire smoke above 10 micrograms per cubic meter.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else about smoke-wave years or the people in those places could explain the difference.
- The paper reports relative changes. Without baseline hospitalization rates, a percentage does not tell you the absolute number of extra cases.
