## Cover

**Kicker:** Future Science OA, global data
**Headline:** Countries that eat more animal protein **also report more dementia**, but mostly in poorer nations.

## Slide 1 - Why this matters

**Title:** Animal protein may track with broader changes in diet and health as countries get richer.

**Basis:** stated    <!-- stated = the paper says it; inferred = our read, must stay conditional -->

This pattern was stronger in poorer nations than in wealthy ones. That suggests animal protein marks a wider shift in how people eat and age.

The finding points to risks that depend on where a country sits in that shift.

## Slide 2 - What they found

**Title:** Researchers linked national animal protein supply to dementia rates in up to 204 countries.

Higher animal protein supply went together with higher dementia rates. The link was steeper at low-to-moderate supply levels, then flattened at the highest levels.

Animal protein supply was also linked to larger female-male gaps in dementia.

## Slide 3 - The setup

**Title:** The team used global data from WHO, World Bank, UN, and food databases to test these links.

They used several methods to compare animal protein supply with dementia rates. They broke down results by income level and region.

The study treated animal protein as a marker of how diets and health risks change as countries grow.

## Caveats

- This study found a pattern, not a cause. Something else could explain the link.
- Country-level data cannot tell us what happens to people who eat more or less animal protein.
- The study did not directly test whether animal protein itself drives dementia, or whether it marks other changes in diet and lifestyle.
