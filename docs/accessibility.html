Accessibility

We want every reader to be able to use this page, including people using a screen reader, keyboard-only navigation, browser zoom or a raised default font size.

How accessible this page is

This site aims to conform to WCAG 2.1 level AA (opens in a new tab), the standard US courts and regulators reference. We believe the site currently meets it.

What that means here in practice

  • Every text and background colour pair on the site is at or above the 4.5:1 contrast ratio AA requires; the lowest is 4.96:1.
  • Everything is reachable and operable with a keyboard alone, and the element you are on is always shown with a visible amber focus ring.
  • A "skip to main content" link is the first thing a keyboard or screen reader user reaches.
  • The page uses real landmarks and headings, and the study list is marked up as a list, so a screen reader can announce and jump through it.
  • Links that open a new tab say so in their accessible name.
  • Text is sized in relative units, so it follows your browser's font size setting as well as zoom.
  • Motion is disabled if your system asks for reduced motion, and borders strengthen if it asks for higher contrast.
  • Nothing on the site depends on colour alone to convey meaning.

Known limitations

Every study links out to a journal or repository we do not control. Once you follow one of those links you are on someone else's site, and we cannot make any promise about how accessible it is.

Study titles and journal names are reproduced exactly as the publisher recorded them. If a publisher's own title contains an unexplained abbreviation or a formatting oddity, it will appear here that way, because silently rewriting a paper's title would be worse.

How this was checked

Contrast ratios are computed from the site's own palette rather than eyeballed. The structural requirements above - the skip link, the landmarks, the list semantics, the focus styles, the new-tab warnings - are each pinned by an automated test that runs on every change to the site, so they cannot be removed by accident later.

This site does not use an accessibility overlay or plug-in widget. Those are widely regarded by disabled users and accessibility practitioners as unhelpful, and they have themselves attracted litigation.

Tell us if something is wrong

If any part of this site is difficult or impossible for you to use, please tell us and we will fix it. This is a one-person project, so the honest promise is: we will reply within a week and tell you what we are doing about it.
